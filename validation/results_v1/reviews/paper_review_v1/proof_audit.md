# Bounded proof audit of pinned Part I

The main results are correct under their stated deterministic, full-state, smooth-domain assumptions. They are direct graph constructions. The source is the previously pinned [13-page v1 PDF](../../../reviews/paper_review_v1/bayer_v1.pdf), with its [source manifest](../../../reviews/paper_review_v1/source_manifest.json). The [15-row ledger](claim_ledger.csv) identifies each scope and qualification.

For q=(x0,u0,…,uN−1), finite recursive inverse images of the open dynamics domain give an open D_N. After a fixed coordinate permutation, Phi_N(q)=(q,G_N(q)). Its derivative contains the identity block I_(n+Nm), projection onto q is a continuous smooth inverse, and the map is an embedding of dimension n+Nm. No controllability or stability assumption is involved. The residual-constraint Jacobian in (x1,…,xN) is block lower triangular with identity diagonal, so the regular-level-set proof yields the same dimension. Fixing x0 pulls back D_N to an open input slice and gives Nm dimensions.

Exact encoder/decoder identities simply project and re-roll the exact dynamics. If a C1 encoder and decoder reconstruct every trajectory exactly, differentiating their composition on the tangent space factors an identity of rank n+Nm through the latent dimension. This proves the bound; it says nothing about approximate compression on a restricted distribution. The decoder domain A in Corollary 4.4 should be described as a smooth domain or via an open-neighborhood extension so that differentiation is unambiguous.

The sampled-flow argument uses smooth dependence on initial conditions and held parameters on an open domain of existence. Fixed-step RK4 is a smooth finite composition only where every stage is admissible. Restricting free inputs to a closed box introduces corners. General state restrictions are not automatically smooth boundaries.

Two qualifications matter for possible extensions. First, with x+=x and y=0, output trajectories carry no information about x0: the IO behavior has dimension Nm, not n+Nm. Remark 5.1 already recognizes this limitation. Finite delay embeddings need observability or generic-embedding hypotheses; [Takens](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~saito/data/synchrosqueezing/takens_detect-strange-attractors-turbulence.pdf) concerns autonomous compact-manifold systems, not arbitrary forced noisy observations. Second, Section 8's noise-coordinate dimension statement requires retaining the noise coordinates or proving their injective effect on the recorded behavior. If f ignores xi, adding free xi does not enlarge the x/u trajectory image. An augmented graph (q,xi,G(q,xi)) does have the enlarged dimension. The wording is underspecified rather than a disproof of the main theorem.

Part I establishes useful explicit behavioral coordinates and separates geometry from finite-data spanning. Its eight-item bibliography contains Willems (three items), Markovsky et al., Willems et al. 2005, Coulson 2019, Lee and Hartman. Direct checks of primary papers confirm the omitted antecedents listed in the [literature matrix](literature_matrix.csv): nonlinear data-driven MPC/DeePC, learned neural dynamics shooting MPC, uncertainty-aware PETS, controlled Koopman MPC and delay embeddings. An omission indicates incomplete positioning; it does not alone prove plagiarism or absence of any novel formulation.

The active code retains x0 and every proposed input, so projection composed with the learned decoder is the identity even for a useless transition network. CEM therefore searches the ordinary Nm-dimensional input space. The graph theorem does not alter the transition loss, optimize uncertainty, establish trajectory fidelity or predict closed-loop success. The closest computational family is [neural dynamics shooting MPC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.02596), with [PETS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.12114) providing a richer uncertainty-aware variant. H adds a five-step queue and an identified LQR switch. This is an assessment of algorithmic structure, not a measured performance verdict.

A potential research gap is a nonlinear IO/noise-aware analogue of exact data-enabled prediction under explicit identifiability and error conditions. Part I flags the IO issue and defers learning/control to Part II; the current full-state deterministic implementation does not close that gap. Comparative benefit, data efficiency and novel-system performance remain unresolved until E0–E4. The AI-assistance disclosure is source metadata only and is not used as evidence against the mathematics.
